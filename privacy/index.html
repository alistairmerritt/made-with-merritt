Second Chair

Second Chair is an iPhone app and a Mac app. The Mac app reads what Music is playing on your Mac. It sends that to your iPhone through a relay server I run, so your iPhone can show it on the Lock Screen and in StandBy. Your taps on the iPhone go back to your Mac the same way. There are no accounts: you pair your iPhone with your Mac using a code.

What the relay holds

While your iPhone and Mac are paired, the relay holds:

  • What's playing on your Mac:
    • the song's title, artist, album, artwork and length;
    • how far through it is, and whether it's playing;
    • Music's volume, and the names of the speakers it can play to (such as "Kitchen HomePod");
    • up to 25 songs from Up Next;
    • when the song plays from a playlist in your library, that playlist's name, how many songs it has and the song's place in it;
    • when the song plays from an album in your library, the album's title and artist, how many songs it has and the song's place in it;
    • whether that playlist or album is shuffled, whether your Mac is shuffling its whole library, and whether the song is from Apple Music outside your library;
    • Music's shuffle and repeat settings;
    • while your Mac plays from Apple Music outside your library, when that started.
    Only the latest is kept, so each update replaces the one before. The relay keeps the artwork of the last 64 songs.
  • Your devices:
    • your Mac's name, as set in System Settings (it often includes your own name);
    • a random identifier for each device, made up by the apps;
    • the app and system versions.
    Your iPhone is named only "iPhone".
  • Push tokens: the addresses Apple gives the iPhone app, so the relay can update your Lock Screen, show your Mac's song and controls in a Live Activity, and, if you turn it on, send the notification when music starts.
  • Pairing keys, stored only in a form that can't be read back.

To update your Lock Screen, the relay sends what's playing to your iPhone through Apple's push notification service.

When you use Continue on Mac, the song's Apple Music ID, title and position go through the relay to your Mac, with what it's playing from, so your Mac can carry on with it: a playlist's name, its Apple Music ID when it has one, how many songs it has, the song's place in it and whether it's shuffled; an album's title, artist, how many songs it has, the song's place in it and whether it's shuffled; or a station's Apple Music ID and name. So do whether your whole library is shuffled, your repeat setting, and whether the playlist or album was just added to your library. The relay passes these on without keeping them.

When you use Continue from iPhone on your Mac and the playlist or album isn't in your library, your iPhone sends its name to your Mac through the relay, so your Mac can ask you whether to add it. The relay passes it on without keeping it. Your answer goes back to your iPhone the same way, and the relay keeps it with that request for up to two minutes.

What Second Chair doesn't do

There are no accounts, and Second Chair doesn't ask for your name or email address. It doesn't keep a history of what you listen to. It has no advertising, analytics or tracking. I don't sell anything about you or share it with anyone, apart from the services that run Second Chair, listed below.

Pairing

Pairing codes expire after five minutes. To stop people guessing codes, the relay counts pairing attempts from each IP address, and deletes each count within an hour.

How long it's kept

Everything the relay holds for a pairing is deleted as soon as you unpair, from either your iPhone or your Mac. If your Mac doesn't connect to the relay for 90 days, for example because you deleted the apps without unpairing, the relay deletes everything for that pairing the same way. To have it deleted sooner, contact me.

On your iPhone and Mac

  • Both apps keep their settings and pairing key on the device.
  • Apple Music on your iPhone: if you allow access, Second Chair reads what Apple Music is playing on your iPhone, the playlists and albums in your library, your recently played list and the songs of a playlist or album in it, and searches the Apple Music catalogue, for Continue Listening. This happens on your iPhone and between your iPhone and Apple. Only the playlist, album or station that's playing goes anywhere else, as described above.
  • Adding to your Apple Music library: when you use Continue on Mac with an Apple Music playlist or album that isn't in your library, Second Chair asks Apple Music to add it to your library, so your Mac can play it. This changes your library: the playlist or album stays there until you remove it. It happens only when you choose Add & Continue, or have chosen Add to Library in the iPhone app's Settings. By default, Second Chair asks first.
  • The Mac app looks songs up in Apple's iTunes Search service, by artist, title and album, to find their artwork and Apple Music IDs.
  • Music on your Mac: once you connect Second Chair to Music for Continue Listening, the Mac app reads your Music library, its playlists and its albums on your Mac, to find each song's Apple Music ID and the playlist or album it plays from. Only what's described above leaves your Mac.
  • Try a Demo, in the iPhone app, uses a simulated Mac on your iPhone and sends nothing to the relay.

Services that run Second Chair

  • Cloudflare runs the relay. What the relay holds is stored on Cloudflare's network and may be stored outside Australia. Cloudflare may keep short-lived technical logs, such as IP addresses, to run and protect its network. Cloudflare's privacy policy
  • Apple delivers the push notifications, provides Apple Music on your iPhone if you allow it, and handles App Store purchases. I don't receive your payment details.
