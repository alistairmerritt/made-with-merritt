Getting started · Mac 0.3.1 and the iPhone beta

Get Second Chair working.

Set up Second Chair on your Mac and iPhone to see and control your Mac’s music from your Lock Screen, Control Centre and StandBy. You’ll need both devices nearby. There are four steps.

Before you begin

  • A Mac with Apple silicon running macOS 27With Music, Apple’s app, on it.
  • An iPhone running iOS 27One iPhone per Mac.
  • An internet connection on both devicesThey don’t need to be on the same network.
  • A TestFlight invitation for Second Chair on iPhoneBuild 202611160929 or later.

Older operating systems and Intel Macs haven’t been tested yet. You don’t need an Apple Music subscription for the main player; only Continue Listening uses one.

1Install Second Chair on your Mac

  1. Mac1a

    Download Second Chair 0.3.1.

  2. Mac1b

    Open the zip file and move Second Chair to Applications.

  3. Mac1c

    Open Second Chair. Apple has notarised it, so it opens without a warning.

  4. Mac1d

    When asked for permission to control Music, click OK.

    Without it, Second Chair can’t see what’s playing.

Second Chair appears as a music note in your Mac’s menu bar. It doesn’t open a separate window.

Recommended: click the music note and turn on Launch at Login, so Second Chair is ready whenever you use your Mac.

2Install Second Chair on your iPhone

  1. iPhone2a

    Open your TestFlight invitation.

    Don’t have one? Ask for an invitation.

  2. iPhone2b

    Install TestFlight from the App Store if you need to, then install Second Chair.

  3. iPhone2c

    Open Second Chair and tap Continue.

You’ll see Pair with Mac.

3Pair your devices

You’ll get a six-digit code from your Mac, enter it on your iPhone, then approve the connection on your Mac.

  1. Mac3a

    Click the music note in the menu bar and choose Pair iPhone…

  2. iPhone3b

    Enter the six-digit code and tap Pair.

  3. Mac3c

    When the menu says your iPhone wants to show this Mac’s music., click Allow.

Your devices are now paired.

The code expires after five minutes and works once. If it expires, choose Pair iPhone… again for a new one.

4Show what’s playing

One last step

Pairing alone doesn’t turn on the Lock Screen player.

  1. MacOpen Music and start playing a song.
  2. iPhoneOpen Second Chair and tap Show in Now Playing.

The button stays unavailable until music is playing on your Mac.

You’re ready.

Lock your iPhone. The song playing on your Mac should appear with its artwork and playback controls.

You’ll also see the player in Control Centre, in landscape StandBy while your iPhone is charging, and on Apple Watch.

The sound stays on your Mac. You can play, pause, skip and seek from any of them, and change the volume of Music on your Mac from the Lock Screen or the Second Chair app.

Second Chair also works when you’re away from home, as long as both devices have an internet connection and your Mac is awake with Second Chair running.

Next: Continue Listening, to take the song with you when you leave your desk.

After setup · needs Apple Music on your iPhone

Continue Listening

Take your music with you, then bring it back. Leaving your desk? Your iPhone picks up the song in Apple Music from the same point, with your Up Next queue, and your Mac pauses. Back at your desk, your Mac carries on where your iPhone got to.

It needs an Apple Music subscription on your iPhone and a one-time connection between Second Chair and Music on your Mac. Home Sharing isn’t required.

aConnect Second Chair to Music, once

  1. Mac

    Click the music note and choose Connect to Music… A four-digit code appears.

  2. Mac

    In Music, select Second Chair under Devices in the sidebar, then enter the code.

  3. Mac

    If macOS asks about the local network, click Allow.

    The menu then says Connected to Music. Your music can move between this Mac and your iPhone.

This code is four digits, typed into Music on your Mac. It isn’t the six-digit code you used to pair your iPhone.

The iPhone app’s Continue Listening section then says Ready. If something isn’t set up, it says what to do: see its statuses.

bTake it with you

  1. iPhone

    In Second Chair, tap Continue on iPhone.

    Apple Music on your iPhone plays the song from the same point, with up to 25 songs from Up Next, then your Mac pauses. The first time, allow Second Chair access to Apple Music.

It’s also an action in Shortcuts. Run it again when nothing has changed on your Mac and your iPhone carries on with what it’s playing, so it suits an automation, such as one that runs when you connect to CarPlay.

cBring it back

Either way, your Mac carries on from where your iPhone got to, and your iPhone pauses.

  1. Mac

    Click the music note and choose Continue from iPhone.

    It’s there whenever Music on your Mac is paused or stopped. After Continue on iPhone it’s the menu’s main button. The menu says Asking your iPhone…, then Your iPhone is handing over…

  2. iPhone

    Or, in Second Chair, tap Continue on Mac.

Continue from iPhone is also an action in Shortcuts on your Mac, and Continue on Mac is one on your iPhone, so bringing the music back can be an automation too, such as one that runs when your headphones connect to your Mac.

If the Mac’s menu says Answer the notification on your iPhone or Apple Watch., tap Continue on Mac on the notification. That needs notifications allowed for Second Chair, which turning on Remind When Music Starts asks for.

Good to know

  • Songs need to be in Apple’s music catalogue. Songs only in your Mac’s library, such as uploads and some purchases, can’t move to your iPhone.
  • If your Mac has fewer than three songs lined up, as on a station, a station made from the song follows on your iPhone.
  • Continue on Mac works for any Apple Music song. If your Mac’s queue doesn’t have it, your Mac plays that song’s station: from the same point when the station starts with it, otherwise from the station’s first song.
  • In the iPhone app, each button is greyed out while the music is already playing on that side.

A few things to know

Keep Second Chair running on your Mac.
It needs to be open to update your iPhone. Turning on Launch at Login is recommended.
Open Music yourself.
Second Chair doesn’t open Music by itself.
Dismissed the player?
Open Second Chair on your iPhone to bring it back. iOS keeps a dismissed player hidden until then. You can also turn on an optional reminder.
Want to hide the player?
Tap Stop Showing in the iPhone app. Your devices stay paired.
Prefer Shortcuts?
Every control is also an action in the Shortcuts app, under Second Chair.

Get reminders

Dismissed the player? Second Chair can tell you when your Mac starts playing again after a break, so the player is a tap away. Turn it on if you’d like it.

  1. iPhonea

    Turn on Remind When Music Starts.

  2. iPhoneb

    Allow notifications when prompted.

After 30 minutes without music, you’ll get a silent notification when playback starts. Tap it to bring the player back. It’s sent as Time Sensitive, so it shows during a Focus.

If the reminder doesn’t appear

Three iPhone settings can still hide it:

  • A Focus with Time Sensitive notifications turned off, in Settings › Focus › the Focus › Apps.
  • Show Previews set to When Unlocked, which hides the text in StandBy.
  • Settings › StandBy › Show Notifications turned off.

Privacy

Second Chair sends the current song, artwork, playback information and the names of your Mac, its speakers and your iPhone through a service run by its developer, so that your iPhone can show and control your Mac’s music. Continue Listening also sends the song’s Apple Music ID and up to 25 songs from Up Next.

Some songs trigger a lookup in Apple’s music catalogue. The apps and the service have no analytics, and you don’t need an account.

Exactly what leaves your Mac
  • Whenever playback changes, to the developer’s relay server and on to your iPhone through Apple’s push notification service: the song’s title, artist, album, length and position, whether it’s playing, the artwork, the volume of Music on your Mac, the names of your Mac’s speakers, and the names of your Mac and iPhone.
  • Only once you’ve connected to Music for Continue Listening: the song’s Apple Music ID and up to 25 songs of Up Next.
  • To Apple’s public catalogue search, only for songs Music has no artwork or Apple Music ID for: the album name, lead artist and country.
  • From your iPhone to Apple Music, for Continue Listening: a request for the song’s station.
  • From your iPhone to your Mac, through the relay, for Continue on Mac with a song your Mac’s queue doesn’t have: the song’s Apple Music ID, its title and how far through it your iPhone is, so your Mac can start the song’s station at the same point.
  • Stays on your Mac: the connection between Second Chair and Music. That’s why macOS asks about the local network. Only whether it’s connected goes to your iPhone, for the Continue Listening status.

The pairing code is random, lasts five minutes and works once. After pairing, each device has its own long random key, and unpairing from either side cancels both.

This website has no analytics and keeps nothing. Its headline typeface comes from Adobe Fonts, so your browser also loads it from Adobe, which counts page views for font licensing. If you try the demo with your own song, what you type goes to Apple’s public catalogue search, and the artwork and 30-second preview come from Apple.

Advanced: to turn off catalogue searches, run this in Terminal on your Mac, then quit and reopen Second Chair:

defaults write com.alistairmerritt.MusicStandBy.Mac catalogArtwork -bool false

Songs Music has no artwork for then show none, and songs played from Apple Music that aren’t in your library can’t be continued on your iPhone.

Disconnecting and removing Second Chair

  • To hide the player without unpairing, tap Stop Showing on your iPhone.
  • To unpair, choose Unpair iPhone in the Mac’s menu, or Unpair Mac in the Mac section of the iPhone app. Either side unpairs both. Unpair before deleting the apps, so the connection is cancelled.
  • To disconnect Continue Listening, choose Disconnect from Music under Continue Listening in the Mac’s menu. Music still lists Second Chair under Devices; Music › Settings › Advanced › Forget All Remotes removes it, along with every other remote.
  • To remove the Mac app, turn off Launch at Login, choose Quit Second Chair, then move it from Applications to the Bin.
  • To remove the iPhone app, delete it like any other app.

Known limits

Second Chair follows only Apple’s Music app on the Mac. One iPhone can be paired per Mac. Both devices need the internet, and your Mac needs to be awake with Second Chair running. Older operating systems and Intel Macs haven’t been tested.

Feedback

On your iPhone, take a screenshot in Second Chair and choose Share Beta Feedback, or open TestFlight and tap Send Beta Feedback. Or get in touch, which works for the Mac app too.
