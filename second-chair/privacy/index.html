Second Chair for Mac and iPhone

Privacy Policy

Second Chair shows the music playing on your Mac on your iPhone. To do that, some details of what’s playing have to travel between them. This page says what, where it goes, and why. There are no accounts, no analytics and no advertising.

Last updated 15 November 2026 · Mac 0.3.0 and the iPhone beta

What Second Chair sends

Your Mac and iPhone don’t talk to each other directly. Second Chair on your Mac sends what’s playing to a small relay server run by Second Chair’s developer, and the relay passes it to your iPhone through Apple’s push notification service. That’s what lets it work away from home.

  • Whenever playback changes: the song’s title, artist, album, length and position, whether it’s playing, the artwork, the volume of Music on your Mac, the names of your Mac’s speakers, and the names of your Mac and iPhone, which are used for pairing.
  • Only if you’ve connected Second Chair to Music for Continue Listening: the song’s Apple Music ID and up to 25 songs of Up Next (title, artist, album, length and ID). The Apple Music ID and Up Next are never put in push notifications.
  • Your commands: play, pause, skip, seek, volume and Continue Listening requests travel back the same way, from your iPhone to your Mac.
  • Reminders: if you turn on Remind When Music Starts, the reminder is sent through Apple’s push notification service.

What goes to Apple

  • Apple’s push notification service delivers playback updates and reminders to your iPhone.
  • Apple’s public catalogue search is asked for a song’s artwork or Apple Music ID when Music on your Mac doesn’t have them. Only the album name, lead artist and your country are sent.
  • Apple Music on your iPhone plays songs for Continue Listening, and Second Chair asks it for a song’s station. That needs your permission the first time, and Apple handles it under its own privacy policy.

What stays on your Mac

The connection between Second Chair and Music, used for Continue Listening, never leaves your Mac. That’s why macOS asks about the local network.

What the relay keeps

For each pairing, the relay keeps the latest playback state and the most recent artwork, up to 64 covers, so your iPhone can fetch them when it wakes.

When you unpair, from either device, the relay deletes everything it holds for that pairing straight away. The relay runs on Cloudflare, whose storage keeps a recovery history for 30 days; Second Chair never uses it, and after 30 days nothing is left. If you delete the apps without unpairing, the pairing and its latest state stay on the relay, so unpair first.

Pairing is protected like this:

  • The pairing code is six random digits, valid for five minutes, used once, and stored only as a hash.
  • After pairing, your Mac and iPhone each get their own long random key, which the relay stores only as a hash.
  • Keys, codes and push tokens are never written to the relay’s logs.
  • Unpairing from either device cancels both keys at once.

Your choices

  • Hide the player: tap Stop Showing in the iPhone app.
  • Stop Continue Listening: choose Disconnect under Continue on iPhone in the Mac’s menu. Up Next is no longer sent.
  • Unpair: choose Unpair iPhone on the Mac, or Unpair in the iPhone app. Do this before deleting the apps.
  • Turn off catalogue searches: see Privacy in the setup guide for the Terminal command.

This website

These pages have no analytics and no forms. The headline typeface comes from Adobe Fonts, so your browser loads it from Adobe, which counts page views for font licensing. If you try the demo with your own song, what you type goes to Apple’s public catalogue search, and the artwork and 30-second preview come from Apple. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages; the Made with Merritt privacy policy covers that, and the contact form.
