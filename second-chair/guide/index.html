Shortcuts and automations

Use Second Chair’s actions in Apple Shortcuts to control playback, move your music between devices or restore the Lock Screen player.

  • Take your music in the carRun Continue on iPhone when your iPhone connects to CarPlay.
  • Bring it back to your deskRun Continue on Mac when your headphones connect.
  • Bring the player backRun Show in Now Playing (the same as Show on Lock Screen in the app) after a playback notification. Your iPhone needs to be unlocked for Second Chair to open. In Live Activity mode it doesn’t show the Live Activity; open Second Chair instead.
How to make an automation
  1. Open Shortcuts on your iPhone, tap Automation, then +.
  2. Choose when it should run, such as CarPlay.
  3. Choose Run Immediately.
  4. Add the Second Chair action you want.

For Continue on iPhone and Continue on Mac, set up Continue Listening first. How to set it up

Every Second Chair action

On your iPhone: Play or Pause on Mac, Play on Mac, Pause on Mac, Next Song on Mac, Previous Song on Mac, Jump to Time on Mac, Set Volume on Mac, Show in Now Playing, Stop Showing, Continue on iPhone and Continue on Mac.

On your Mac: Continue from iPhone.

Player Display

Choose how your Mac’s music appears on your iPhone, in Second Chair’s Settings › Lock Screen and StandBy › Player Display.

Automatic (the default)
Prefers Now Playing, with a Live Activity when your Mac resumes after a break. Opening Second Chair restores Now Playing.
Now Playing
Apple’s full music player on your Lock Screen, in the Dynamic Island and StandBy, and on Apple Watch. If iOS replaces it, open Second Chair to bring it back.
Live Activity
Your Mac’s music and controls in a Live Activity on your Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island, instead of Now Playing.

The Live Activity shows the song’s artwork, title and artist, with buttons to play, pause and skip. Under Automatic, tapping it opens Second Chair, which brings Now Playing back and closes the Live Activity.

Live Activity limits
  • On a locked iPhone, its buttons ask for Face ID first.
  • iOS ends a Live Activity after 8 hours.
  • Once you swipe it away, it stays away until you open Second Chair or your Mac next resumes after a break.
  • With Player Display set to Live Activity, there’s no full player in Control Centre, and no scrubber or volume control.

Notifications

Under Automatic, a Live Activity appears when your Mac starts playing after a break, so most people won’t need a notification. If the Live Activity can’t show, Second Chair can notify you instead.

  1. iPhoneIn Second Chair, tap the gear for Settings, then turn on Notify When Music Resumes.
  2. iPhoneAllow notifications when asked.

You’ll then get a silent notification when your Mac starts playing after 8 minutes or more without music, whenever the Live Activity can’t show: with Player Display set to Now Playing, or with Live Activities turned off for Second Chair. Tap it to bring the player back.

If the notification doesn’t appear

The notification is Time Sensitive, so it can appear during a Focus when those notifications are allowed. These iPhone settings can affect what you see:

  • A Focus with Time Sensitive notifications turned off, in Settings › Focus › the Focus › Apps.
  • Show Previews set to When Unlocked, which hides the text in StandBy.
  • Settings › StandBy › Show Notifications turned off.

Everyday tips

Keep Second Chair running on your Mac.
Turn on Launch at Login in the music note’s gear (⚙︎) menu to start Second Chair when you log in.
Open Music yourself.
Second Chair doesn’t open it for you.
Player gone from your Lock Screen?
Open Second Chair on your iPhone to bring it back. Under Automatic, a Live Activity usually appears when your Mac resumes after a break; tap it to bring the player back. If the Live Activity can’t show, turn on notifications to bring it back with a tap.
Want to hide the player?
In the iPhone app, open Settings and choose Stop Showing. This also stops the Live Activity. Your devices stay paired.
Away from home?
It works anywhere both devices are online, as long as your Mac is awake with Second Chair running.

What comes with you

Continue Listening usually picks up the same song from the same point, carrying over your repeat setting. Your playlist, album, station or upcoming songs can come with you too, with the differences below.

When a station moves to your Mac, Apple Music chooses the starting song. If Second Chair creates a station from your current song instead, it usually starts with that song.

Apple Music works a little differently on Mac and iPhone. Here’s what carries over in each direction.

What you’re listening toContinue on iPhoneContinue on Mac
A playlist in your librarySame playlist, including shuffleSame playlist, including shuffle
An album in your librarySame album, including shuffleSame album, played in order
Your library on shuffleLibrary shuffle continuesLibrary shuffle continues
An Apple Music playlist or album not in your librarySame playlist or albumAdded to your library first, then played*
A stationSame song and stationSame station, starting on a song Apple chooses
Songs added with Play NextUp to 25 upcoming songs, instead of the playlist or albumNot transferred

* Requires Add Playlists and Albums to Library (Settings › When Continuing on Mac) to be turned on. More about it

Where Mac and iPhone differ
  • Shuffle order: each device shuffles on its own, so the order can change.
  • Shuffled albums: albums continued on your Mac play in their original order.
  • Play Next: songs added with Play Next on your Mac take priority over the playlist or album. Your iPhone receives up to 25 upcoming songs in the same order, then stops. Songs added with Play Next on your iPhone don’t transfer to your Mac.
  • Recently started music: Apple Music can take around a minute to recognise a playlist, album or station that isn’t in your library. Until then, Second Chair may use upcoming songs or a station based on the current song.
  • Shuffling part of your library: an artist, genre or other selection shuffled on your iPhone continues on your Mac as your whole library on shuffle. iOS doesn’t tell apps which selection was shuffled. In the other direction, an artist shuffled on your Mac becomes up to 25 upcoming songs on your iPhone; other selections become your whole library on shuffle.
  • Just changed shuffle or repeat on your Mac? Your iPhone learns of it within about 15 seconds, so continuing straight away can use the old setting.
  • Songs outside Apple Music: uploaded songs and some purchases can’t be continued on your iPhone.
Adding music to your library

Some Apple Music playlists and albums need to be in your library before your Mac can play them.

On your iPhone, open Second Chair’s Settings › When Continuing on Mac and turn on Add Playlists and Albums to Library. Second Chair can then add these playlists and albums when you continue on your Mac. The setting is off by default. Anything added stays in your library until you remove it.

With the setting off, your Mac plays a station based on the current song instead.

What Continue Listening needs
  • An Apple Music subscription on your iPhone. Viewing and controlling playback in the Music app on your Mac doesn’t require a subscription. Home Sharing isn’t needed.
  • Second Chair for Mac 0.3.5 or later and the current iPhone app.
  • Second Chair connected to Music on your Mac. Without this connection, continuing on your Mac falls back to a station based on the current song.
  • For playlists and albums in your library, Sync Library turned on on both devices.
If Continue Listening isn’t ready

This connects Second Chair to Music on your Mac. It’s separate from the six-digit device pairing code you enter in Second Chair on your iPhone.

Something not working?

Find your issue below for steps to fix it.

The player

Nothing appears on your Lock Screen

Check that Music is playing on your Mac, then open Second Chair on your iPhone. If it still doesn’t appear, open Settings in the app and tap Restore Now Playing.

Show on Lock Screen is greyed out

Start playing something in Music on your Mac.

The player has disappeared, or Apple Music shows instead

Open Second Chair on your iPhone, or tap its playback notification. Under Automatic, a Live Activity usually appears when your Mac resumes after a break; tap it to bring the player back.

iOS keeps a dismissed player hidden. After your Mac has been paused for about 8 minutes, iOS may show your iPhone’s own Apple Music instead. Resuming playback on your Mac doesn’t switch it back.

Now Playing and a Live Activity both show

Open Second Chair, which closes the Live Activity.Under Automatic, your Mac resumed before iOS had replaced Now Playing.

No Live Activity appears

On your iPhone, check Settings › Second Chair › Live Activities. When they’re off, Second Chair’s Settings say Live Activities are turned off for Second Chair in Settings. In Live Activity mode, opening Second Chair shows it again.

The Live Activity’s buttons ask for Face ID

That’s how iOS handles buttons on a locked iPhone. Glance at your iPhone or unlock it.

Your iPhone says your Mac is offline

Wake your Mac and make sure Second Chair is running. Check that both devices are online, then tap Reconnect on your iPhone.

No artwork for a song

Music has no artwork for this song, and Apple’s catalogue search found no close match. Playback still works without artwork.

Privacy and details

Second Chair sends playback information, artwork and your devices’ names through a relay service run by its developer to show your Mac’s music on your iPhone. Continue Listening also sends the information needed to resume playback, such as the playlist or album and upcoming songs.

There are no analytics, and you don’t need an account. Read the privacy policy

Exactly what leaves your Mac
  • Whenever playback changes, to the developer’s relay server and on to your iPhone through Apple’s push notification service: the song’s title, artist, album, length and position, whether it’s playing, the artwork, the volume of Music on your Mac, the names of your Mac’s speakers, and the names of your Mac and iPhone.
  • Only after you’ve connected to Music for Continue Listening: the song’s Apple Music ID, up to 25 songs from Up Next, and Music’s shuffle and repeat settings. For a playlist or album in your library, also its name, the album’s artist (for albums), its song count, the current song’s place in it and whether it’s shuffled. Second Chair also sends whether your Mac is shuffling its whole library and whether playback is from Apple Music outside your library. For playback outside your library, it sends when that playback started.
  • To Apple’s public catalogue search, only for songs Music has no artwork or Apple Music ID for: the album name, lead artist and country.
  • From your iPhone to Apple Music, for Continue Listening: requests for the song’s station, your recently played list, and the songs in a playlist or album from that list. These identify what your iPhone is playing and the song’s place in it.
  • From your iPhone to Apple Music, to add to your library, only for Continue on Mac and only while Add Playlists and Albums to Library is on: a request to add an Apple Music playlist or album to your library, so your Mac can play it. This changes your library.
  • From your iPhone to your Mac, through the relay, for Continue on Mac when your Mac can’t skip ahead to the song in its own queue: the song’s Apple Music ID, title and playback position. This also includes what it’s playing from: a playlist’s name, Apple Music ID (if it has one), song count, current song’s place and shuffle state; an album’s title, artist, song count, current song’s place and shuffle state; or a station’s Apple Music ID and name. It also includes whether your whole library is shuffled, your repeat setting, and whether the playlist or album was just added to your library. The relay passes this information on without keeping it.
  • Stays on each device: your other playlists and albums. Each device reads its own library to find the song, and only the playlist or album that’s playing is named.
  • Stays on your Mac: the local connection between Second Chair and Music. That’s why macOS asks for local network access. Second Chair shares this connection’s status with your iPhone for the Continue Listening status; playback information travels through the relay as described above.

The six-digit device pairing code is random, lasts five minutes and works once. After pairing, each device has its own long random key. Unpairing from either side cancels both keys.

This website uses no site analytics. Its headline typeface comes from Adobe Fonts, so your browser loads it from Adobe, which counts page views for font licensing. If you try the demo with your own song, what you type goes to Apple’s public catalogue search; the artwork and 30-second preview come from Apple.

Advanced: to turn off catalogue searches, run this in Terminal on your Mac, then quit and reopen Second Chair:

defaults write com.alistairmerritt.MusicStandBy.Mac catalogArtwork -bool false

With catalogue searches off, songs without artwork in Music show no artwork in Second Chair. When you continue an Apple Music song that isn’t in your library, your iPhone looks it up in Apple Music itself. Upcoming songs outside your library are left out.

Known limits

Second Chair requires an Apple silicon Mac running macOS 27 and an iPhone running iOS 27. It follows only Apple’s Music app on the Mac.

You can pair one iPhone with each Mac. Both devices need an internet connection, and your Mac must be awake with Second Chair running.

A Live Activity lasts up to 8 hours. After that it ends until you open Second Chair or your Mac resumes after a break.

Disconnecting and removing Second Chair
  • To hide the player without unpairing, choose Stop Showing in Settings in the iPhone app. This also stops the Live Activity.
  • To unpair, choose Unpair iPhone… in the gear (⚙︎) menu on your Mac and confirm, or Unpair Mac in Settings › Mac in the iPhone app. Either action unpairs both devices. Unpair before deleting the apps to cancel the connection.
  • To disconnect Continue Listening, choose Disconnect from Music in the gear (⚙︎) menu on your Mac. Second Chair remains listed under Devices in Music. To remove that entry, use Music › Settings › Advanced › Forget All Remotes. This removes every remote, including any others you use.
  • To remove the Mac app, turn off Launch at Login and choose Quit Second Chair, both in the gear (⚙︎) menu, then move it from Applications to the Bin.
  • To remove the iPhone app, delete it like any other app.
