Shortcuts and automations

Second Chair works with Apple Shortcuts. Use its controls in your own shortcuts, or automate them to run when you need them.

  • Take your music in the carRun Continue on iPhone when your iPhone connects to CarPlay.
  • Bring it back to your deskRun Continue on Mac when your headphones connect.
  • Bring the player backRun Show in Now Playing when Second Chair’s notification arrives. Skip the tap
How to make an automation
  1. Open Shortcuts on your iPhone, tap Automation, then +.
  2. Choose when it should run, such as CarPlay.
  3. Choose Run Immediately.
  4. Add the Second Chair action you want.

Continue Listening needs to be set up first. How to set it up

Every Second Chair action

On your iPhone: Play or Pause on Mac, Play on Mac, Pause on Mac, Next Song on Mac, Previous Song on Mac, Jump to Time on Mac, Set Volume on Mac, Show in Now Playing, Stop Showing, Continue on iPhone and Continue on Mac.

On your Mac: Continue from iPhone.

Skip the tap

Want Second Chair to return to your Lock Screen and StandBy without opening the app yourself? A simple Shortcuts automation can restore Now Playing when the notification arrives.

Get the shortcut

Once set up, the automation will open Second Chair once your iPhone is unlocked. Now Playing returns to your Lock Screen and StandBy when your iPhone locks again.

It needs Remind When Music Starts turned on.

Or set it up yourself
  1. Open Shortcuts → Automation → + and select Notification.
  2. Choose Second Chair.
  3. Tap Add Filter and set the notification message to contains Tap to show it on your Lock Screen. This ensures the automation only responds to playback notifications, not other Second Chair notifications.
  4. Select Run Immediately and turn off Notify When Run.
  5. Add Second Chair’s Show in Now Playing action.

Notifications

Second Chair can notify you when your Mac starts playing again, so you can bring the player back to your Lock Screen with a tap.

  1. iPhoneIn Second Chair, turn on Remind When Music Starts.
  2. iPhoneAllow notifications when asked.

After 8 minutes without music, you’ll get a silent notification when your Mac starts playing again. Tap it to bring the player back, or skip the tap with a shortcut.

If the notification doesn’t appear

It’s sent as Time Sensitive, so it shows during a Focus. These iPhone settings can still hide it:

  • A Focus with Time Sensitive notifications turned off, in Settings › Focus › the Focus › Apps.
  • Show Previews set to When Unlocked, which hides the text in StandBy.
  • Settings › StandBy › Show Notifications turned off.

Away from home?

What comes with you

Continue Listening picks up the same song from the same point, with your playlist, album or station and your repeat setting.

One exception: a station moved to your Mac keeps going, but Apple Music starts it on a different song. If Second Chair creates a station from your current song instead, it usually starts with that song.

Apple Music works a little differently on Mac and iPhone, so some details change. Here’s how each kind of music carries over.

What you’re listening toContinue on iPhoneContinue on Mac
A playlist in your librarySame playlist, including shuffleSame playlist, including shuffle
An album in your librarySame album, including shuffleSame album, played in order
Your library on shuffleLibrary shuffle continuesLibrary shuffle continues
An Apple Music playlist or album not in your librarySame playlist or albumAdded to your library first, then played*
A stationSame song and stationSame station, starting on a song Apple chooses
Songs added with Play NextUp to 25 upcoming songs, instead of the playlist or albumNot transferred

* Requires Automatically Add to Library to be turned on. More about it

Where Mac and iPhone differ
  • Shuffle order: each device shuffles on its own, so the order can change.
  • Shuffled albums: albums continued on your Mac play in their original order.
  • Play Next: if you’ve added songs with Play Next on your Mac, your iPhone gets up to 25 of your Mac’s upcoming songs, in the same order, instead of the whole playlist or album, and stops after them. Songs you add with Play Next on your iPhone don’t transfer to your Mac.
  • Recently started music: Apple Music can take around a minute to recognise a playlist, album or station that isn’t in your library. Until then, Second Chair uses the upcoming songs or a station based on the current song.
  • Shuffling part of your library: shuffling an artist, a genre or another selection on your iPhone continues on your Mac as your whole library on shuffle, because iOS doesn’t tell apps what a shuffle came from. From your Mac, an artist on shuffle comes to your iPhone as up to 25 upcoming songs, and other selections continue as your whole library on shuffle.
  • Just changed shuffle or repeat on your Mac? Your iPhone learns of it within about 15 seconds, so continuing straight away can use the old setting.
  • Songs outside Apple Music: uploaded songs and some purchases can’t be continued on your iPhone.
Adding music to your library

Some Apple Music playlists and albums need to be in your library before your Mac can play them.

Turn on Automatically Add to Library, under Continue Listening in the Second Chair app on your iPhone, and Second Chair adds them for you. It’s off by default, and anything added stays in your library until you remove it.

With the setting off, your Mac plays a station based on the current song instead.

What Continue Listening needs
  • An Apple Music subscription on your iPhone. Home Sharing isn’t needed.
  • Second Chair for Mac 0.3.5 and the current iPhone app.
  • Second Chair connected to Music on your Mac. Without it, your Mac plays a station based on the current song.
  • For playlists and albums in your library, Sync Library turned on on both devices.
If Continue Listening isn’t ready

Continue Listening in the iPhone app starts with a Status. When it isn’t Ready, it says what to do:

Privacy and details

To show your Mac’s music on your iPhone, Second Chair sends what’s playing, its artwork and the names of your devices through a service run by its developer. Continue Listening also sends what’s needed to pick up where you left off, such as the playlist or album and what’s coming up next.

There are no analytics, and you don’t need an account. Read the privacy policy

Exactly what leaves your Mac
  • Whenever playback changes, to the developer’s relay server and on to your iPhone through Apple’s push notification service: the song’s title, artist, album, length and position, whether it’s playing, the artwork, the volume of Music on your Mac, the names of your Mac’s speakers, and the names of your Mac and iPhone.
  • Only once you’ve connected to Music for Continue Listening: the song’s Apple Music ID and up to 25 songs of Up Next. When the song plays from a playlist or album in your library, also its name (and an album’s artist), how many songs it has, the song’s place in it and whether it’s shuffled. And whether your Mac is shuffling its whole library, whether the song is from Apple Music outside your library, and Music’s shuffle and repeat settings, and, while your Mac plays from Apple Music outside your library, when that started.
  • To Apple’s public catalogue search, only for songs Music has no artwork or Apple Music ID for: the album name, lead artist and country.
  • From your iPhone to Apple Music, for Continue Listening: requests for the song’s station, for your recently played list, and for the songs of a playlist or album in it, to tell what your iPhone is playing and where the song is in it.
  • From your iPhone to Apple Music, to add to your library, only for Continue on Mac and only while Automatically Add to Library is on: a request to add an Apple Music playlist or album to your library, so your Mac can play it. This changes your library.
  • From your iPhone to your Mac, through the relay, for Continue on Mac when your Mac can’t just skip ahead to the song in its own queue: the song’s Apple Music ID, its title and how far through it your iPhone is. Also what it’s playing from: a playlist’s name, its Apple Music ID when it has one, how many songs it has, the song’s place in it and whether it’s shuffled; an album’s title, artist, how many songs it has, the song’s place in it and whether it’s shuffled; or a station’s Apple Music ID and name. And whether your whole library is shuffled, your repeat setting, and whether the playlist or album was just added to your library. The relay passes these on without keeping them.
  • Stays on each device: your other playlists and albums. Each device reads its own library to find the song, and only the playlist or album that’s playing is named.
  • Stays on your Mac: the connection between Second Chair and Music. That’s why macOS asks about the local network. Only whether it’s connected goes to your iPhone, for the Continue Listening status.

The pairing code is random, lasts five minutes and works once. After pairing, each device has its own long random key, and unpairing from either side cancels both.

This website has no analytics and keeps nothing. Its headline typeface comes from Adobe Fonts, so your browser also loads it from Adobe, which counts page views for font licensing. If you try the demo with your own song, what you type goes to Apple’s public catalogue search, and the artwork and 30-second preview come from Apple.

Advanced: to turn off catalogue searches, run this in Terminal on your Mac, then quit and reopen Second Chair:

defaults write com.alistairmerritt.MusicStandBy.Mac catalogArtwork -bool false

Songs Music has no artwork for then show none. When you continue a song from Apple Music that isn’t in your library, your iPhone looks it up in Apple Music itself, and upcoming songs that aren’t in your library are left out.

Known limits

Second Chair follows only Apple’s Music app on the Mac. One iPhone can be paired per Mac. Both devices need the internet, and your Mac needs to be awake with Second Chair running. Older operating systems and Intel Macs haven’t been tested.

Disconnecting and removing Second Chair
  • To hide the player without unpairing, tap Stop Showing on your iPhone.
  • To unpair, choose Unpair iPhone in the Mac’s menu, or Unpair Mac in the Mac section of the iPhone app. Either side unpairs both. Unpair before deleting the apps, so the connection is cancelled.
  • To disconnect Continue Listening, choose Disconnect from Music under Continue Listening in the Mac’s menu. Music still lists Second Chair under Devices; Music › Settings › Advanced › Forget All Remotes removes it, along with every other remote.
  • To remove the Mac app, turn off Launch at Login, choose Quit Second Chair, then move it from Applications to the Bin.
  • To remove the iPhone app, delete it like any other app.
